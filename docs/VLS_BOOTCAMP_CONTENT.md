## 2. The problem
Heading: From Constitutional Theory to Courtroom Understanding.
Studying Fundamental Rights, constitutional provisions and landmark principles is essential. But understanding how they operate through legal proceedings requires another level of clarity.
When is a writ petition appropriate? How does a Special Leave Petition differ from an ordinary appeal? What is the purpose of a review petition? How do the jurisdiction and powers of the Supreme Court and High Courts differ?
The boot camp is designed to connect constitutional concepts with the petitions, remedies and procedures encountered in legal practice.
Closing: Knowing the provision is the beginning. Understanding its practical application is the next step.

## 3. Curriculum
Heading: Explore Constitutional Law Through Six Core Learning Areas.
01 Constitutional Foundations — Understand Fundamental Rights, Directive Principles of State Policy, Fundamental Duties, constitutional doctrines, Union and State institutions, Centre–State relations, emergency provisions and constitutional amendments.
02 Writ Jurisdiction & Public Interest Litigation — Study writ petitions under Article 226, the Madras High Court Writ Rules, 2021, constitutional writ remedies, writ miscellaneous petitions, filing procedures, categorisation of proceedings and PILs.
03 Election Petitions — Explore the legal framework for election disputes involving legislative bodies and local authorities, including MLA, MLC, Lok Sabha, Rajya Sabha, Panchayat and Municipal elections.
04 Original Jurisdiction of the Supreme Court — Learn about the Supreme Court's original jurisdiction concerning constitutional disputes between the Union and States, or between States.
05 Special Leave Jurisdiction — Understand the purpose and constitutional framework of Special Leave Petitions before the Supreme Court under Article 136.
06 Appellate Jurisdiction & Constitutional Appeals — Explore civil and criminal appeals before the Supreme Court and High Courts, constitutional appellate remedies, and the relationship between different levels of judicial review.

## 4. The 13 key topics
Heading: Constitutional Remedies & Petition Practice.
Support: Explore the major constitutional remedies and petition categories referenced in the boot camp, including their purpose, relevant jurisdiction and role in legal proceedings.
Design: three visual clusters, NOT 13 identical cards.

Cluster A — Writs & Original Remedies
- Writ Petitions & Writ Appeals — Constitutional writ remedies and related appellate procedures
- Public Interest Litigations (PILs) — Proceedings concerning public-interest matters
- Original Petitions — Original-jurisdiction matters before the appropriate Supreme Court or High Court forum
- Supervisory Petitions — High Court supervisory jurisdiction under Article 227

Cluster B — Appeals & Corrective Remedies
- Special Leave Petitions (SLPs) — Discretionary special leave jurisdiction of the Supreme Court
- Constitutional Appeals — Appeals involving constitutional questions and appellate remedies
- Review Petitions — Applications seeking review of judicial decisions under applicable law and rules
- Curative Petitions — The exceptional curative jurisdiction of the Supreme Court
- Certificate Petitions — Constitutional certification relating to appeals under Article 134A

Cluster C — Special Constitutional Proceedings
- Election Petitions — Statutory challenges involving election disputes
- Advisory Reference — The Supreme Court's advisory jurisdiction under Article 143
- Transfer Petitions — Procedures for transferring cases between appropriate courts
- Contempt Petitions — Proceedings relating to contempt of court

(4 + 5 + 4 = 13)
